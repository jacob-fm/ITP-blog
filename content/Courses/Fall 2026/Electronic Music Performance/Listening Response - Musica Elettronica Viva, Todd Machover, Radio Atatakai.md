## Musica Elettronica Viva - Spacecraft (1967)

https://youtu.be/dzxLoCwiEy0?si=7uaxc5t5ND514fNp

I wish I was tapped in enough to appreciate this sort of thing, but I honestly don't see the appeal. I did enjoy the sort of "quiet after the storm" effects as the piece winds down, but I don't (yet) see the appeal in the screeching and scratching. The texture of the voice modulated by the synth is kind of interesting, but as a whole package I did not care for this.

## Todd Machover - Begin Again Again

https://youtu.be/GZvMjlNBnmA?si=i-BMWwSpNYCFO--x

As we talked about in class, it can be difficult for an experimental performance to be compelling without a visual cue for the audience of what is happening. While I enjoyed the song enough as a piece of music, I was able to find a video excerpt of Yo-Yo Ma's performance of the piece, which was a bit more interesting. It still suffers from the fact that it just appears as him playing a cello, so it is impossible for the audience to intuit what exactly he's doing to trigger all of the "hyper" parts of the performance.

## Radio Atatakai: Cul-de-sac

I listened to one of the live-streams from [wavefarm.org](wavefarm.org) called Radia: Cul-de-sac by Radio Atatakai. When I first opened the stream, there was a really lovely melody playing that had this intermittently buzzy feedback tone. After a moment, that faded out, and two people began (or resumed, I think), their conversation. The conversation was really difficult to understand. After a bit I understood that they were performing a radio play, although quite half-heartedly so that they seemed to be kind of laughing at the script as they were reading it. That plus their thick (Swiss?) accents + the content of the script made it hard to follow, but I did at some point pick up that the characters were supposed to be in a car together. This was weird and off-putting in a way that kind of tickled me, for a bit, but I did get bored of it after a bit. I do wish I could figure out what song they were playing at the start.
