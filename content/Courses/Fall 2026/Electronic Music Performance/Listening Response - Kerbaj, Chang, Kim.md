## Mazen Kerbaj - Starry Night

I found this very compelling. From a high-level view, the premise of the performance, a "duet" with live bombs as they drop on your city, conveys the helplessness felt by the civilians forced to suffer these attacks. More granularly, I was impressed with the way Kerbaj plays his instruments in relation to the bombs. At times he makes a convincing call-and-response with the explosions, while other times he shows restraint in letting the explosions ring out on their own. There really was a musical quality to the exchange. 
This piece reminded me of [[Samson Young]]'s [Nocturne](https://www.thismusicisfalse.com/nocturne/), which is also a sound-based performance which incorporates real warfare. I find both of these quite unsettling in the way that you can almost forget that the warfare being represented is quite real, and real people are dying in these recordings.

## Leo Chang - Jeonmonori

I didn't really care for this one, to be honest. In the beginning, when he's swinging his head around, it just seemed to me like there was a big mismatch in tone. The music, lighting, and Chang's body language all suggest an atmosphere of solemnity, yet in the center of the screen a man is swinging around a big rope from the top of his hat. Maybe if the sounds he was producing were more interesting to me, I would've gotten more out of this, but for the most part it seemed generic, like what I think of when I imagine "ambient droning". I don't really listen to drone music though, so I could be way off-base.

## Jin Hi Kim - Touching the Moon

After finding the last piece a bit too generic, I really enjoyed how eery, playful, and weird this sounded. The performance flows in between harmonic and dissonant or abrasive moments. I thought about being in a strange trance or trip.
