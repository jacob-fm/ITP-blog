## Prompt

*“SURPRISE: It is not an openness as in candor, but an openness as in vulnerability.” (Finite and Infinite Games, pg. 18)*

## Design Experience

### Ideation

Colloquially, when someone opens up about intimate details, we describe that as "vulnerability". Carse would describe that as merely "candor", whereas true vulnerability is not just when one is open to describing things that they view as true about themselves, but when they are open to their self being changed by new events.

With this in mind, we thought about how we could actually *change* our classmates in 15 minutes. Maybe, we could change their relationships between one another, by engineering an opportunity for them to become closer with one another. We were reminded of the [36 Questions to Fall in Love](https://www.remento.co/journal/36-questions-to-fall-in-love-or-at-least-bring-you-closer-together), a pop-psychology phenomenon born out of a [research paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167297234003) from 1997. 

To simply split up our participants and hand them these questions might induce *candor*, but for a chance at *vulnerability*, we decided that after some time going through these questions in pairs, we would ask everyone to write a challenge to their partner, based on their conversation. The hope was that participants would learn something about their partner's hopes and aspirations while making their way through the questions, and they would give their partner a small push to help them achieve those things.

### Concrete Details

Of the 36 questions, we carefully chose 7 that we put in the "icebreaker" category, and 7 for the "getting deeper" category. These are as follows:

#### Icebreakers

- Given the choice of anyone in the world, whom would you want as a dinner guest?
- If you were able to live to the age of 90 and retain either the mind or body of a 30-year-old for the last 60 years of youaaar life, which would you want?
- Make three true "we" statements each. For instance, "We are both in this room feeling..."
- Would you like to be famous? In what way?
- For what in your life do you feel most grateful?
- Share an embarrassing moment in your life.
- Your house, containing everything you own, catches fire. After saving your loved ones and pets, you have time to safely make a final dash to save any one item. What would it be? Why?
 
 #### Getting Deeper
- If a crystal ball could tell you the truth about yourself, your life, the future, or anything else, what would you want to know?
- Is there something that you've dreamed of doing for a long time? Why haven't you done it?
- Tell your partner something that you like about them already.
- Complete this sentence: "I wish I had someone with whom I could share..."
- What is your most treasured memory?
- If you were going to become a close friend with your partner, please share what would be important for them to know.
- If you knew that in one year you would die suddenly, would you change anything about the way you are now living? Why?

We made each category its own "deck", and came up with contrasting designs for them.

We gave each pair both decks simultaneously. Our only instructions were to go through the cards they were given at their own pace. In order to induce intimacy within the pairs, we thought it best to remove ourselves as much as possible from the experience. We designed the cards so that without explicitly needing to explain anything, the participants would understand that the first set of cards was "lighter", and the second set was "heavier", and they could choose how they wanted to proceed with that in mind. We expected that most would start with the "icebreaker" deck and later move on to the "getting deeper" deck, but wanted to allow for some pairs who were already familiar with one another to go straight for the "getting deeper" deck if they so choose.

## Day-of

### Observation

#### Arrival & seating

- People came in groups rather than in a line, and many were already chatting before the
activity started.
- After being seated, some continued whispering.
- Two pairs chose to sit on the chairs, as expected.
- One participant noticed me observing and said, “I feel like being watched.” LOL.
- Some people waited for instructions, while others started exploring the cards
immediately.
#### Beginning of the activity
- Laughter started almost immediately.
- Around 2 minutes in, people were already browsing through the cards.
- Some asked, “Should we open and see?”
- People were sitting casually, almost like at a picnic.
- Some seemed confused and stared at their cards silently for a while without talking.
#### Getting into the conversation
- Around 5 minutes in, I started hearing more personal stories, e.g.:
- “When I was in 4th grade...”
- “I was traveling in xxx...”
- People seemed to loosen up a bit, judging by their gestures and facial expressions.
#### Halfway
- People started browsing through the cards again; I saw/heard someone say, “Let’s skip.”
- Around 60% of people were holding purple cards, 20% green, and 20% unknown.
- After the halfway announcement, people went silent for a bit.
- I heard someone say, “I love your voice, it’s very gentle.”
- One pair didn’t seem to enjoy the activity as much. One of them kept looking around and
staring at the cards on the floor.

#### Challenge

- After the announcement, there was more laughter.
- One minute in, only about half of the participants had started writing; the rest were still
talking.
- One pair leaned against each other’s backs while writing — CUTE!
- There seemed to be different ways of handling the challenge in a trio.
#### Wrapping up
- There was laughter after people switched cards.
- I wondered how the trio would write/do the challenge.

### Reflection

#### Instructions & activity flow

- There was some confusion about how to start. Should indicate more clearly: “Please
start talking.”
- Going straight into deep questions and then doing ice-breakers might not work well.
Ice-breakers could lead into deeper questions instead.
- Some people mentioned pressure while thinking about the challenges to be given out.
- Favor of open-ended questions were mentioned.
- One pair only did one question;
- One trio laid all the cards open ;
- One pair shuffled and mixed the two decks.
#### Questions & depth
- The setup of the questions, atmosphere, and environment seemed to contain
comfortable elements that helped people open up.
- The level of depth was surprising and meaningful.
- Some participants seemed to be thinking about what to write and what would feel
good/genuine.
#### Timing & challenge
- Writing time was not enough.
- Different group structures may require different ways of distributing the challenge,
especially for trios.
