## GERP 1

We were put into a group of 7 and tasked with ordering an appetizer, main, and dessert.

we were given black cards, which give each person a specific limit on the way they can communicate (mine said I could only speak chinese or use gestures). We were given white cards, face down, which gave us each a secret individual objective. And we were given an orange card which had a group goal (ours was to order 3 things from different countries).

We went around the table and each person did their best to advocate for the foods they wanted.

Different communication abilities meant some players were more capable of advocating than others. But still, for simple things like "lets get this food" I could just point to it.

Some players tried to give reasons for one food or another like "this one is too expensive" or "this one is too spicy".

Since we didn't have any budget or real food to worry about, everyone's concerns were purely arbitrary. There was no reason to be convinced of anything other than if it didn't interfere with your goal. My goal was very simple: get the pineapple pizza. So I just pointed to it on the menu. I was frustrated there wasn't more to do, or more interesting reasons/things to talk about.

Interpreting in terms of "Play as capture":

I understand that we were "captured" in the sense of being forced to limit our communication. In a way this capture is what enabled the playing, but I'm not sure if that's really the case. The game would largely function the same if we could just talk freely, but maybe less interestingly.

## GERP 2

We were each given a communication restriction and a hint. We all had a shared goal to identify the key item in the room that would allow us to win. I was given the hint "velcro" and my restriction was not being able to say more than 3 words at a time.

Right after the game started, I saw a guy in the corner of the room waving wildly, so I approached him, and eventually he pointed to his own hat, then to one of the moderators, and beckoned me to go to him, so I understood I should go grab the moderators hat. That was the correct item, and we won.

I clarified with one of the moderators before time started that after 3 words, I should wait for someone else to speak before I could say another 3 words. The guy in the corner wasn't speaking at all, so I didn't say anything at all either until I retrieved the hat, and said "Did we win?" At that time I thought he might be another moderator still. The game ended after just 3 or 4 minutes if I had to guess.

It seemed like it would have been more fun to actually get to engage with the other communication rules. One guy could only say the opposite of what he wanted to say, another had to say "just kidding" after every sentence. While it was thrilling to be instrumental in winning the game, I felt like I missed out on the core fun of the experience.

In this one we were more directly "captured" in the sense that this was framed as an escape room. But there wasn't actually any freedom gained since there was nothing that really made me consider whether I could leave or not. Once the game ended, I didn't leave anyway since we did the activity in the auditorium and there was no reason to leave.
